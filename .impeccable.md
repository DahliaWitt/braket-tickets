# Braket Community Design Context

## Users

**Primary audience**: Queer and trans people in the underground techno/electronic music scene. They are finding events, getting vetted into private communities, purchasing tickets, and managing their membership.

**Context of use**: Often on mobile, often late at night or in dark environments. Pre-event (buying tickets, checking details), at-event (showing QR codes at the door), and post-event (checking account, managing resales). Admin users manage events, check-in guests, and handle vetting applications.

**Job to be done**: Get into events run by and for their community. The vetting process protects the safety of trans/queer spaces. The platform is the gate — it should feel like it belongs to the community, not like corporate infrastructure.

## Brand Personality

**Three words**: Raw, Queer, Underground.
**Aesthetic north star**: Berlin warehouse culture — the textures, the lighting, the density, the lack of polish.

## Voice & Copy

The brand voice is one voice with a volume knob, not separate personalities. It comes from the same place everywhere — a friend in the scene who's warm, a little cheeky, and genuinely cares about you having a good time. The visual design is raw and textured; the voice is warm.

**Core tone**: Warm, direct, conspiratorial. Like a friend who's genuinely excited telling you about something. Confident without being promotional. Intimate without being exclusive. Playful without being unserious about things that matter.

**How it sounds**:

- "We've got something for your mind, your body, and your soul."
- "Bring a snack and your best dancing shoes, because we're going all night."
- "Take some tylenol and make it to sunrise: it'll be worth it."
- "iykyk… if not, be prepared for a wild ride :)"

**Volume knob by context**:

| Context                                 | Volume                                     | Example                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Event descriptions                      | Full warmth, poetic, sensory               | "a night of connection and community uniting visionary DJs and the coolest freaks you know" |
| Emails & notifications                  | uwu, soft, playful, lowercase energy       | existing templates in `convex/email/templates.ts`                                           |
| UI copy (buttons, labels, empty states) | Warm but concise — a wink, not a monologue | "no events yet — go make some noise" not "No events found."                                 |
| Safety & inclusion statements           | Direct, unflinching, no corporate hedging  | "Transphobia... will not be tolerated and will result in immediate ejection"                |
| Error messages                          | Helpful and human, never robotic           | "something went wrong on our end — try again?" not "An unexpected error occurred"           |
| Admin/settings UI                       | Clear and friendly, less poetic            | still warm, just practical                                                                  |

**Key patterns**:

- **Second person, present tense** — "we're bringing," "you won't want to miss," "bring your beautiful self"
- **"By and for"** — the recurring motif. Events/spaces are made _by_ the community _for_ the community
- **Sensory and physical language** — "stomping feet," "mandatory ass shaking," "sweaty techno," "punishing closer"
- **DJ/artist bios read like introductions at a house party** — you're meeting a friend, not reading a press kit
- **Logistics are warm, not corporate** — "bring a snack and your best dancing shoes" not "food and proper footwear recommended"
- **Playful innuendo is welcome** — sex-positive without being explicit: "a playroom to put up your decency ;)"
- **Political solidarity stated plainly** — not buried, not hedged, not performative
- **NOTAFLOF** mentioned naturally — accessibility is part of the culture, not a checkbox
- **Casual punctuation** — semicolons used playfully, em dashes for asides, :) and ;) are fine
- **Kaomoji are welcome** — use them as a warm, playful signature in emails, confirmations, and celebratory states: `:3`, `(^_^)`, `\o/`, `(｡•̀ᴗ-)✧`, `(っ˘ω˘ς)`. Treat them like seasoning, not confetti: one strong kaomoji is better than five stacked ones. Do not use them to minimize safety, identity, payment, or account-security stakes.
- **No underscores between words** — use spaces in all UI copy
- **No `//` prefix on UI text** — it reads as a code comment, giving a programmer/hacker aesthetic that clashes with the brand. The monospace fingerprint already carries the retro-futurist tone; `//` tips it into coder territory.

**Sacred-space copy ethics**:

- Do not exploit, trivialize, commercialize, or commodify the underground, queer/trans community safety, vetting, intimacy, or sacred spaces.
- Never frame access as a purchasable identity, initiation, scene credential, or shortcut into "the underground." Braket supports communities; it does not sell belonging.
- Avoid copy that turns subculture into a product promise: "verify and you're in," "join the underground," "unlock the scene," "exclusive access," "VIP underground," or anything that sounds like selling entry to something sacred.
- Use "underground" descriptively and sparingly, not as a conversion hook. Prefer concrete, respectful language about the actual action: verifying an email, finishing vetting, buying a ticket, checking event details, or receiving a payout.
- If playful copy touches safety, vetting, money, identity, or community trust, turn the volume down. Warmth is good; irreverence that cheapens the stakes is not.

**Anti-patterns** (what Braket copy should NEVER sound like):

- Corporate/SaaS: "We're excited to announce," "streamline your experience," "No events found."
- Code-comment aesthetics: `// Section Title`, `/* label */` — the brand is underground warehouse, not a terminal
- Press releases or Resident Advisor listings
- Corporate DEI language: "we celebrate diversity," "committed to fostering an inclusive workplace"
- Influencer-speak: "obsessed," "literally dying," "slay"
- Scene-as-product copy: "Welcome to the underground — verify and you're in," "unlock the underground," "exclusive access to queer nightlife"
- Detached third-person descriptions
- AI filler: "comprehensive," "robust," "seamless," "ensures," "leverage," "utilize"
- Overly literary purple prose

**Emotional arc**:

- **Pre-login / public pages**: Community warmth — "this was made by people who know why these spaces matter." Not pretentious, not access-bait, and never selling the underground as a commodity. A wink is fine; a conversion funnel into sacred space is not.
- **Post-login / dashboard**: Community belonging — "these are your people." Safe, identity-affirming, warm. The app feels like it's _yours_.
- **Event pages**: Full expression — poetic, sensory, excited. This is where the voice lives loudest.
- **Checkout / transactional**: Still warm but clear — you're helping someone get to the party, not selling them a product.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone**: DIY avant-garde with warmth. Textured surfaces, bold type, high contrast. Think Berlin warehouse party — raw concrete, low light, deep color — not Silicon Valley startup. The app should feel like a digital extension of the event flyers: distressed textures, stage-lit depth, print-culture density.

**Major influence — Psychedelia**: The visual language draws from psychedelic design traditions — 60s/70s concert posters, liquid light shows, op-art, and the warped/saturated aesthetic of modern psych-electronic culture: organic warping and distortion, saturated color that feels like dye or light (not digital gradients), heavy texture and grain, chromatic aberration, moiré-like interference. The acid test for any effect: **does it feel analog and physical — like light projected through oil wheels onto a warehouse wall — or like a Photoshop filter?** If it's the filter, it's out. This test overrules any motif list, including this document's.

**What the flyers actually do** (the observable brand language — when in doubt, imitate this):

- **Lowercase display event names** — blackletter for recoherence, warped rounded sans for decoherence. Display faces rotate per event series; the app holds steady on Syne.
- **Heavy condensed uppercase for artist names**, separated by interpuncts ("S4M23 · FAITED").
- **Lowercase monospace for all metadata** — dates, times, locations, taglines: "june 20  all vinyl, all night", "a flinta* techno party", "secret sf location".
- **Banded print layouts** — full-width bands (white/cream against black), hairline rules, table-like listing frames. Structure comes from print, not from cards and elevation.
- **Sharp corners everywhere.** There is not a single rounded corner on any flyer.
- **Data as artwork** — the waterfall texture is quantum experiment data; the decoherence flyer is a waveform plot. Scientific origin material, treated with intention, not decoration.
- **Halftone/dither and duotone photo treatment** — photography is screened, dithered, and duotoned (the December flyer's purple dither), never a clean full-color photo drop.
- **Grain and dust on everything** — distressed paper, film grain, speckle.
- **Edge fringing lives in the artwork** — thin chromatic rims around texture shapes, slight riso-style ink bleed on saturated display type. These are properties of the *printed image*, never CSS effects applied to UI controls.
- **Per-event accent rotation** — burnt orange one night, hot pink/violet the next, pure B&W the next. The app does NOT rotate: it distills the flyer universe into the fixed plum/violet/amber palette. Never import a one-off flyer accent into the UI.

**Theme**: "Pulp"

**Palette direction** (exact values live in `frontend/src/styles.css` tokens):

- **Primary**: Deep plum/wine — derived from the rich berry tones in event photography and flyer backgrounds
- **Secondary**: Violet — cooler purple, from the December flyer's duotone and the waterfall texture's cool rims
- **Accent**: Burnt amber/orange — the warm tone from the waterfall texture on the June flyer
- **Dark mode background**: Warm near-black with plum undertone (not cold/blue-shifted)
- **Light mode background**: Cool neutral white/gray (warmth comes from accent colors, not surfaces)
- **Killed colors**: Neon Pink, Acid Green, Electric Violet — too electric/screen-lit for the *app*. (Flyers may run hot pink for a night; the UI does not.)
- Both modes are first-class. Dark mode is where the brand hits hardest, but light mode must work, not just exist.

**Typography system**:

- **Syne** (display/headings) — geometric with off-kilter character, underground art-scene pedigree. Max weight 800; `font-black` (900) does not exist for Syne.
- **Inter** (body) — neutral, readable, lets display + mono carry the personality
- **Space Mono** (labels/data/code) — editorial retro-futurist monospace with real character

**Typographic signature**: `font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-2xs` — this pattern appears on 400+ elements and IS the brand's visual fingerprint.

**Decorative motifs** (all verified against the flyers):

- Waterfall texture overlay at 30% opacity
- Chromatic aberration / color offset on display type (the June flyer's "recoherence" layering)
- Analog noise/grain texture overlays (distressed paper, film grain, dust)
- Halftone/dither and duotone treatment on photography
- Hairline rules, frames, and full-width bands as layout structure

**Killed motifs** (the original draft reverse-engineered these from AI-generated UI and listed them as brand motifs — do not reintroduce; see the reject-on-sight list under Anti-references):

- ~~Gradient text (plum → violet) for emphasis~~ — no flyer has it; display type is flat color with chromatic *offset* at most.
- ~~Multi-layered glow / neon tube aesthetic~~ — the flyers' "glow" is rims and ink bleed baked into artwork, never CSS box-shadows on controls. UI emphasis comes from weight, contrast, and borders.
- ~~Floating cards~~ — nothing floats on a flyer; depth comes from texture layering, not elevation.

**Backdrop blur policy**: `backdrop-blur-md` (12px) on static overlays (modal backdrops, panels over textured backgrounds) and `backdrop-blur-sm` (4px) on scroll-attached elements (sticky headers). The frosted-glass effect provides depth separation over the waterfall texture — it's material, not elevation. Only remove blur if a measured performance regression is found on a specific element — never as a blanket policy.

**Anti-references** (what this must NOT look like):

- Generic startup SaaS (gradient hero sections, stock photos, rounded-everything, 8px radius on everything)
- Eventbrite / corporate ticketing (template-y, overly functional, no soul)
- Overly polished editorial sites (Resident Advisor — too clean for this brand)
- AI-generated aesthetics — reject these on sight:
  - gradient text (`bg-clip-text`)
  - neon box-shadow glows on buttons/charts/cards
  - floating elevated cards over blurred blobs
  - uniform `rounded-xl`/`rounded-2xl` on everything
  - emoji used as icons
  - Title Case + exclamation-mark copy ("Welcome Back!")
  - JetBrains Mono / Fira Code in UI contexts
  - CSS glitch effects
  - cookie-cutter component-library look

**Imagery**:

- Photography first — real event photos, crowd shots, silhouettes, blurred movement in low light. No stock photography, no illustrations, no AI-generated images.
- Event flyers are the primary visual asset. They set the tone for the brand more than any UI component does. When in doubt about aesthetic direction, look at the flyers.
- The waterfall texture is derived from real quantum experiment data — it's not decorative, it's origin material. Treat it with intention.

**Reference universe**: Underground techno flyers, Berlin warehouse culture, avant-garde web design, zine culture, print design.

## Design Principles

### 1. Character Over Cleanliness

The brand has grit. Every design decision should ask: "does this feel like it belongs on a gig poster or a corporate dashboard?" Choose the poster. Imperfection is a feature — distressed textures, tight tracking, monospace labels, and dense layouts all reinforce the underground identity.

### 2. The Mono Signature

`font-mono uppercase tracking-widest` is the brand's DNA. It appears on labels, badges, buttons, status indicators, and metadata. Treat it as sacred — every new UI element should consider whether its labels should use this pattern. It's what gives the app its underground editorial feel, distinct from generic SaaS.

### 3. Both Modes, Full Commitment

Dark mode is where the brand hits hardest, but light mode is not an afterthought. Both must feel intentional and complete. The palette (plum, amber, violet) was chosen to work at full depth on dark and full saturation on light. Design for dark first, then verify light — never ship one broken.

### 4. Community Safety Through Design

The vetting system, access controls, and identity features aren't just functional — they communicate that this space is protected. Design choices around these features should feel intentional and secure, not corporate-checkbox. The gate should feel human, not bureaucratic.

### 5. Accessible Without Compromise

WCAG 2.2 AA minimum, including 24px minimum touch targets. Reduced motion support. High contrast ratios. Skip links and focus rings. Accessibility is non-negotiable — the community includes people with disabilities, and exclusion by design contradicts the platform's values. Never sacrifice accessibility for aesthetics.

## Component Conventions

- **ZardUI** component library with CVA variants (`zType`, `zSize`, `zShape`)
- **Tailwind CSS v4** with `@theme` blocks (CSS-centric, no JS config)
- **Lucide icons** via `z-icon` component (71+ curated icons)
- **Spacing rhythm**: 16px (`gap-4`, `px-4`) as the primary unit, 24px (`gap-6`) for section-level breathing room. Denser than typical SaaS — closer to print layout — but still readable.
- **Border radius**: **0–4px only.** `--radius` (`0.25rem`, 4px) is the ceiling for everything — cards, dialogs, and empty states included (decided 2026-07-11). Dead-sharp `rounded-none` is welcome and encouraged for structural elements; the sharp/4px mix is intentional and part of the print language. The previous "rounded-xl container exception" was reverse-engineered from AI-generated code and is dead — uniform soft rounding is the fastest way to make the app look AI-generated.
- **Borders**: hairline print-style rules, frames, and bands are on-brand structure (see the decoherence flyer). A border on every card in a grid is not — grids separate cells with gap + background tint.
- **Color tokens**: semantic tokens only (`primary`, `secondary`, `accent`, `destructive`, `success`, `warning`, `info` + their `-foreground`/`-text` variants). Never raw Tailwind palette classes or hardcoded hex in templates. On self-tinted surfaces (`bg-success/10` etc.), use the AA-calibrated `-text` variants. Never alpha-modify a calibrated text token (`text-warning/70`) and never dim text with `opacity-*` — the tokens are contrast-calibrated with near-zero headroom.
- **Transitions**: 200ms cubic-bezier for interactions, 400ms for entrances
- **Focus rings**: 2px solid primary with 2px offset and box-shadow halo (an a11y affordance, not decoration — this is the one sanctioned "glow")
