# Base Rules

Universal technical writing rules. Priority-stacked: Tier 1 overrides all others on conflict.

<rules>

## Tier 1 — Never Violate

1. **Define your audience before writing.**
   *Readers cannot judge relevance without knowing who the document targets and what it assumes.*

2. **State the document's scope and any prerequisites upfront.**
   *Readers who discover halfway through that they lack context will abandon the document.*

3. **Focus each sentence on a single idea.**
   *Compound sentences force readers to untangle multiple concepts simultaneously, increasing misunderstanding.*

4. **Define unfamiliar terms on first use, or link to an existing definition.**
   *Undefined jargon causes the cognitive equivalent of a null pointer error in readers' heads.*

5. **Use active voice for procedures and instructions.**
   *Active voice makes the actor and action explicit, so readers know exactly who does what.*

## Tier 2 — Strong Preference

6. **Keep sentences under 26 words.**
   *Shorter sentences read faster, maintain better, and introduce fewer failure points.*

7. **Use one term for one concept throughout the document.**
   *Inconsistent naming forces readers to guess whether two terms mean the same thing.*

8. **Keep list items parallel in grammar, category, capitalization, and punctuation.**
   *Parallel structure lets readers absorb patterns instead of decoding irregularities.*

9. **Start numbered list items with imperative verbs.**
   *Imperative verbs turn list items into clear, direct instructions.*

10. **Write task-based headings that describe what the reader will do.**
    *Headings built from unfamiliar terminology force readers to read the section before understanding its purpose.*

11. **Introduce information progressively — simple concepts before complex ones.**
    *Readers build mental models incrementally; skipping ahead to advanced material causes confusion.*

12. **Write the caption before creating an illustration, and place it after the figure.**
    *Captions that explain the takeaway focus reader attention and prevent decorative-only graphics.*

## Tier 2b — Accessibility (enforced when applicable)

13. **Write descriptive alt text for every non-decorative image.**
    *Screen readers depend on alt text; without it, visual content is invisible to some readers.*

14. **Use meaningful link text that makes sense out of context.**
    *Screen reader users navigate by jumping between links; "click here" conveys nothing.*

## Tier 3 — Default Behavior

15. **Cut filler words and phrases ("in order to" -> "to", "it should be noted that" -> drop it).**
    *Extra words dilute meaning without adding information.*

16. **Choose simple words over complex synonyms ("use" over "utilize", "start" over "commence").**
    *Simple words reduce cognitive load and translate better.*

17. **Replace ambiguous pronouns (it, they, this, that) with the noun they reference.**
    *Ambiguous pronouns force readers to search backwards for the referent.*

18. **Use tables when comparing two or more items across shared attributes.**
    *Tables let readers scan structured data faster than equivalent prose.*

19. **Introduce every list and table with a sentence that states what it contains.**
    *Context-setting introductions prevent readers from guessing a list's purpose.*

20. **Write sample code that is correct, concise, and runnable.**
    *Broken or bloated code samples erode reader trust and waste debugging time.*

21. **Choose strong, specific verbs over weak ones (raise, generate, trigger — not occur, happen, is).**
    *Precise verbs carry meaning; generic verbs force readers to infer the action.*

## Tier 4 — Nice to Have

22. **Use inclusive, bias-free language.**
    *Inclusive language welcomes the widest possible audience without distraction.*

23. **Replace directional references ("the image above", "the section below") with explicit labels.**
    *Screen readers and reflowed layouts do not preserve spatial relationships.*

24. **Write in second person, present tense ("you configure" not "the user will configure").**
    *Second person addresses the reader directly, which improves engagement and clarity.*

25. **Limit each paragraph to one topic, with the key point in the opening sentence.**
    *Lead sentences let readers decide whether to invest in the full paragraph.*

</rules>
