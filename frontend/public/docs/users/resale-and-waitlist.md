# Resale and waitlist

Plans change. Resale lets you pass your ticket to someone who wants it, and the waitlist makes sure people who missed out get a shot when tickets come back.

## Listing a ticket for resale

If you can't make it to an event, you can list your ticket for resale from your Tickets page. The ticket goes back on sale at the original price — no markups, no scalping.

To list a ticket:

1. Go to your Tickets page
2. Find the event
3. Click the resale option on the ticket you want to list

Your ticket stays valid until someone buys it. If nobody does before the event, it's still yours.

## Cancelling a listing

Changed your mind? Cancel your resale listing anytime before someone buys it. Your ticket goes back to normal, no harm done.

## How resale purchases work

When someone buys a resale ticket, a few things happen:

- The buyer gets a new ticket and QR code.
- Your original ticket is deactivated.
- You get refunded the ticket price.

Resale tickets are sold one at a time, even if the buyer would normally be able to purchase multiple. The price is always whatever the original ticket cost.

## Waitlist notifications

If an event is sold out, you can subscribe to resale notifications. When a ticket gets listed for resale, you'll get an email letting you know it's available.

To subscribe, visit the event page for any sold-out event and look for the notification option. You can unsubscribe anytime.

Fair warning: getting notified doesn't reserve a ticket. It's first come, first served. If someone else grabs it before you, you'll stay subscribed for the next one.

## Limitations

Guest tickets can't be resold — you need an account. You also can't set your own price; tickets always resell at face value (no scalping). And resale has to be enabled by the event organizer, so not every event supports it.
