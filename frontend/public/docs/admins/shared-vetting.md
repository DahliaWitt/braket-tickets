# Shared vetting

Shared vetting lets communities trust each other's vetting decisions. If your community has a trust link with another community, people who are already vetted there can get auto-approved when they apply to yours.

## Why use it

The underground music scene is full of overlapping communities. Someone vetted into one collective is probably fine in a sister collective too. Shared vetting saves everyone the time of re-answering the same kinds of questions — while still giving you control over which communities you trust.

## How trust links work

A trust link is a one-way relationship: "we trust community X's vetting." If someone is vetted in X and applies to your community, they get approved automatically.

One-way means you can trust another community without them trusting you back, and vice versa. Both communities have to independently decide to trust each other if you want it to go both ways.

## Managing trust links

Go to the **Shared Vetting** tab in your Community Admin dashboard. From here you can:

- Add a trust link to another community
- Remove a trust link you no longer want

Adding a trust link doesn't notify the other community or require their approval. You're just telling Braket: "if someone is vetted there, they're good here too."

Removing a trust link stops future auto-approvals. People who were already auto-approved keep their membership.

## What applicants see

Applicants don't see trust links directly. They just apply normally, and if they happen to be vetted in a trusted community, the approval happens instantly. From their perspective, the admin just reviewed their application really fast.

Trust links are about vetting decisions, not membership — approving someone through shared vetting makes them a full member of your community. Worth reviewing your links occasionally, too. Communities change, and a link you set up a year ago might not reflect your current standards.

Auto-approved applications still show up in App History, so you can always see who came in through shared vetting.
